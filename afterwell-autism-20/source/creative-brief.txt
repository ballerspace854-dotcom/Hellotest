THE KID AND THE SKIN
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
- Kids aged about 3–10, real-photo style, natural light, phone-camera feel. Faces can be turned away or partly cropped.
- Show autistic-kid moments that parents instantly recognize: noise-cancelling headphones, a chewy necklace, a fidget toy, a visual schedule chart on the wall, lining up toys, pulling away from touch, covering ears, a weighted blanket, the same favourite shirt.
- Eczema must be IMPOSSIBLE to miss at thumbnail size: angry red or dark patches, thick, cracked, scaly, flaking skin, fresh scratch lines, on elbow creases, behind knees, wrists, hands, neck, cheeks.
- Hard limits: no blood, no open wounds, no oozing, no before/after skin comparisons, and never cream on broken skin.

━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
HOW TO WRITE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
- 5th-grade words. Headline 3–9 words, huge and bold. One support line, 12 words max. Small AFTERWELL logo. Jar always visible.
- Talk about AUTISTIC KIDS directly in the ad ("autistic kids", "sensory kids", "kids on the spectrum").
- NEVER say "your autistic child", "your child's eczema", or "if you're a parent of an autistic kid". Meta rejects ads that say the viewer or their kid has a condition. Talk about autistic kids, not about the viewer.
- First-person parent lines are OK as headlines ("He can't tell me it itches."), but no names, no stars, no "verified buyer", and no fake text or Reddit screenshots.
- Open a loop: put two things together that don't seem to fit, so the parent has to stop and work it out.
- Say less, mean more: show the moment, don't explain the feeling.
- The parent is never the bad guy. The enemy is the itch, the greasy cream, the smell, the bedtime fight.
- Allowed product claims: soothes and moisturizes dry, itchy, eczema-prone skin · light, non-greasy feel · soaks in fast · no added fragrance · no steroids · sea moss, aloe vera, oats · contains oats, patch test first.
- Banned: heals, cures, treats, clears, "no more flare-ups", timelines, doctor approval, and anything saying it helps autism itself.

Make every ad look different from the one before it: change the scene, the layout and the hook type.
Size: 1080 × 1350.

PROBLEM-AWARE ADS (start with the moment they live every day)
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━
AD 1 · Can't say it itches
"He can't tell me it itches. He shows me."
Scene: a nonverbal boy with headphones on, scratching a raw-looking, scaly inner elbow; the parent's hand nearby with the jar.

AD 2 · Sensory feel
"To autistic kids, cream can feel like slime."
Support: "So most creams get wiped right off."
Scene: a macro of a greasy ointment smeared on a sleeve; the child pulling the arm away.

AD 3 · Broken routine
"Bath. Pajamas. Cream. Meltdown."
Scene: a visual schedule chart on the wall with four picture cards; the "cream" card is crumpled. A child's cracked hands are below it.

AD 4 · Sameness
"Same routine every night. Except the cream part."
Scene: a neat bedtime setup with lined-up toys; the child turned away, holding an arm with a red patch.

AD 5 · Clothes vs. cream
"Same shirt all week. No cream, ever."
Scene: a child in a favourite dino shirt, sleeve pulled up to show a severe flaky wrist.

AD 6 · The sensory list
"Tags. Seams. Loud rooms. Now cream."
Support: "Sensory kids already deal with a lot."
Scene: a flat-lay of cut-out clothing tags, headphones and the jar, with a child's dry, cracked hand.

AD 7 · Sleep workaround
"Waiting until they fall asleep to put cream on?"
Scene: a dark room, star nightlight, a sleeping child under a weighted blanket with a scaly wrist out; the parent's hand with the jar.

AD 8 · Itch and overwhelm
"The itch never stops. Neither does the overwhelm."
Scene: a child covering both ears on the floor; an inflamed patch on the back of the knee is visible.

AD 9 · Stim or itch?
"Is it a stim, or is it the itch?"
Support: "Sometimes the rubbing means dry, itchy skin."
Scene: a child rubbing a forearm over and over; a close-up shows a cracked, flaky patch.

AD 10 · School
"Hard to sit still when your arms won't stop itching."
Scene: a classroom table with a fidget toy; the child scratching an elbow crease instead of drawing.
