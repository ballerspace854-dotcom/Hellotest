2. WHO WE'RE TALKING TO
────────────────────────
Parents (moms and dads, 25–45) of a child aged about 2–10 who has eczema AND hates having cream put on. The child is often autistic or sensory-sensitive. These parents are problem-aware and solution-aware: they've seen dermatologists, used prescribed steroids, and tried Aveeno, Eucerin, CeraVe, Cetaphil, Vanicream and Aquaphor. Their bathroom cabinet is full of half-used tubes. They are exhausted and sceptical, not naive.

Their real pain (from parent research, use it, don't quote names):
- The child wipes cream off the second it goes on.
- Parents wait until the child is asleep to apply it.
- Thick, greasy creams make clothes and hair stick; to a sensory kid lotion can feel like "slime or dirt"; a gentle touch can feel like a lot.
- Meltdowns at cream time; bedtime starts with a fight.
- Scratching all night; rough sheets in the morning; the child can't focus at school.
- "Unscented" products that still smell.
- Being scared to try another cream after a bad reaction.
- Feeling judged when people stare at the child's skin.
- Steroids work, but parents ration them.

What they want: a cream their child will actually LET them put on and leave on, so the skin stays moisturized and calmer and the child can sleep, play and learn.

What Afterwell is: a soothing daily moisturizing cream with sea moss, aloe vera and oats. It has a light, non-greasy feel, no added fragrance, and no steroids. It's applied as a thin layer on dry, itchy patches, morning and night.

3. THE ECZEMA MUST BE VISIBLE AND HARD TO LOOK AWAY FROM
────────────────────────
Last round the skin looked basically fine. That kills the hook. In every ad where skin appears, the eczema must be unmistakable and severe at thumbnail size:
- Angry, inflamed red patches on light skin, or dark brown, purple or grey patches on darker skin.
- Thickened, leathery, cracked, scaly, flaking texture with visible white flakes, rough edges, and fresh scratch lines.
- Real locations: inner elbow creases, behind the knees, wrists, backs of hands, neck, cheeks, ankles.
- Show the itch in action: fingernails mid-scratch, a sleeve pushed up to reveal the patch, a hand rubbing it.
- Real photography: natural light, phone-camera realism, real skin pores. Not glossy AI skin.

HARD LIMITS on skin (so Meta approves the ad):
- No blood, open wounds, oozing, weeping or infected-looking skin, and no gore.
- No side-by-side before/after or "results" comparisons of skin.
- Never show cream applied to broken skin.

────────────────────────
4. COPY RULES (Meta policy + honest claims)
────────────────────────
- NEVER call out the viewer's or their child's condition. Banned: "For parents of autistic kids", "Your autistic child…", "Your child's eczema…", "Do you have…". These get ads rejected.
- Instead, describe the SITUATION or the CHILD'S PREFERENCE:
  - "Waiting until they're asleep to put cream on?"
  - "For kids who can't stand the feel of lotion."
  - "When cream time ends in a meltdown."
  - You MAY describe the product as "made with sensory-sensitive kids in mind."
- Allowed claims ONLY: soothes and moisturizes dry, itchy, eczema-prone skin · light, non-greasy feel · absorbs fast · no added fragrance · steroid-free · with sea moss, aloe vera and oats · gentle for daily use.
- BANNED claims: heals, cures, treats, clears, stops or ends eczema or breakouts; any time-bound result ("in 7 days", "first night"); "dermatologist approved"; "safe for newborns"; anything that implies it helps autism.
- NO fake proof: no star ratings, reviews, testimonials, customer names, "verified buyer", review counts, or screenshots made to look like a real person's text, post or comment.
- Name competitor brands nowhere. Generic "other creams" or unbranded tubes are fine.
- Where an angle is about reactions or new creams, add the small line "Contains oats. Patch test first." It builds trust.

Text on each ad: 1 hook headline (max 9 words, huge, readable on a phone in 1 second) + 1 short support line + a small AFTERWELL logo. No paragraphs. High contrast. Navy (#1E2A5A), white and soft blue palette to match the jar.

5. FORMAT
────────────────────────
- 1080 × 1350 (4:5) unless an ad says otherwise.
- Mix these styles across the set so no two adjacent ads look alike: lo-fi phone photo; bold text-over-image; split-screen problem vs. product; flat-lay; extreme macro close-up; handwritten note or sticky note; bathroom-cabinet "graveyard"; bedtime night scene; "us vs. other creams" feature checklist (unbranded); demo shot with a timer.
